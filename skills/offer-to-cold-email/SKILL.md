# Offer to Cold Email

> **Source & credit:** The principles in this skill are adapted from the YouTube video
> **"Give me 24 min and I'll make your cold emails impossible to ignore"** by **Aaron Shepherd**
> ([GrowthFlare](https://www.youtube.com/@AaronxShepherd)) — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N5ORVjBPlcg
>
> All credit for the underlying ideas goes to Aaron; this skill simply packages them for reuse.

## Overview

This skill turns a service into a low-friction front-end offer and then into a short cold email sequence.

The core principle: prospects do not care about the provider's service yet. They care about what happens if they reply.

A strong cold email offer answers:

> If I answer this email, what do I get?

The goal is not to explain the whole service. The goal is to create enough curiosity and perceived value to start a conversation that can naturally lead to a sales call.

## When to Use

Use this skill when the user asks for any of these:

- Create a punchy offer for their service
- Turn a coaching, consulting, agency, or productized service into a front-end offer
- Write cold email copy around an offer
- Create a testimonial-exchange, pilot, teardown, audit, or sprint offer
- Improve a vague offer so it becomes specific and reply-worthy
- Generate follow-ups for a cold outbound sequence

Do not use this for:

- Long-form landing pages
- Full brand positioning projects
- Generic marketing copy without an outbound/email angle
- Email deliverability or technical campaign setup

## Source Principles

Use these principles when creating offers and emails.

1. The front-end offer is not the main service.
   - Bad: I help companies with coaching.
   - Better: I will help your founding team clear the decisions and action items you have been avoiding for 30 days.

2. The offer should be specific, tangible, and easy to say yes to.
   - Specific result
   - Clear timeframe
   - Low risk
   - Low effort
   - Obvious reason why it is valuable

3. The offer should create a "why not?" reaction.
   - Can I send you 100 verified contacts?
   - Can I record a 2-minute teardown?
   - Can I help you clear your avoided action items for 30 days?

4. If the offer is free or unusually generous, explain why.
   - In exchange for a testimonial
   - Because I am building case studies
   - Because I am opening 3 trial spots
   - Because I want proof in this niche

5. The email should be short.
   - No "hope you are well"
   - No long intro
   - No explaining the full service
   - No premature "book a call"
   - No vague claims
   - No fake personalization

6. The CTA should be easy.
   - Worth sending?
   - Want me to send it over?
   - Open to trying this?
   - Should I record one for you?
   - Would this be useful?

## Required Inputs

Ask for or infer:

1. User's main service
2. Target customer
3. Pain point or avoided problem
4. Desired result
5. Timeframe
6. Proof, if available
7. Risk reversal, if available
8. Why the user can make a generous offer
9. Preferred tone: direct, premium, playful, contrarian, founder-to-founder

If the user gives enough context, do not ask questions. Proceed with assumptions and label them.

## Offer Discovery Process

Generate offers in five categories.

### 1. Done-for-you Diagnostic

The user gives the prospect a useful analysis, teardown, audit, or plan.

Examples:

- I will review your founder team's operating rhythm and send back the 3 places you are losing speed.
- I will record a 5-minute teardown of the decisions your team is probably avoiding.
- I will map the action items that are stuck between you and your cofounder.

### 2. Free Implementation Sprint

The user helps create an actual result over a short period.

Examples:

- I will help your founding team clear the action items you have been avoiding for 30 days.
- I will run one weekly founder-accountability sprint for your team. If it helps, give me a testimonial.
- I will help you make and execute the 3 decisions that are slowing the company down.

### 3. Risk-Reversed Paid Offer

The user frames the core service with a guarantee, performance condition, or testimonial exchange.

Examples:

- If we do not create visible momentum in 30 days, do not pay.
- If your cofounder meetings do not get sharper after 4 weeks, you owe me nothing.
- If this does not save you at least 5 hours of founder churn per week, do not continue.

### 4. Curiosity Asset

The user sends something the prospect wants to inspect.

Examples:

- I put together a 1-page founder alignment checklist. Want me to send it?
- I made a simple scorecard for spotting cofounder drift. Want a copy?
- I can send you the operating rhythm I use with 2-founder teams.

### 5. Pattern Interrupt Offer

The user leads with a sharp observation or contrarian angle.

Examples:

- Most 2-founder teams do not have a strategy problem. They have an avoidance problem.
- Your biggest bottleneck probably is not hiring, product, or sales. It is the decisions no one wants to own.
- I help founders finish the uncomfortable work that keeps getting postponed.

## Offer Evaluation Rubric

Score each offer from 1-5 on:

1. Specificity: Is the result concrete?
2. Low friction: Can the prospect say yes easily?
3. Believability: Does it sound plausible?
4. Relevance: Does it hit a painful current problem?
5. Conversation potential: Does it naturally lead to a sales call?
6. Differentiation: Does it sound unlike generic coaching or consulting?
7. Risk reversal: Is there a clear "why not?" element?

Prioritize offers that score high on specificity, low friction, and conversation potential.

## Email Writing Rules

The first email should usually be 1-4 lines.

Structure:

1. Subject line
2. Relevant opener
3. Front-end offer
4. Low-friction CTA

Avoid:

- Hope you are well
- I am reaching out because
- I wanted to introduce myself
- We help companies
- Would you be open to a 30-minute call?
- Long paragraphs
- Big claims without proof
- Generic increase revenue / productivity / alignment language

## Subject Line Rules

Subject lines should be:

- 2-4 words
- lowercase
- curiosity-inducing
- relevant but incomplete
- non-salesy
- like an internal note

Examples:

- founder alignment
- quick question
- avoided action items
- cofounder rhythm
- decision bottleneck
- 30 day sprint
- founder friction
- stuck priorities

Avoid:

- Grow your business
- Coaching opportunity
- Free consultation
- Unlock your potential
- Increase productivity

## Email Templates

### Template 1: Direct Offer

Subject: [short curiosity subject]

[First name] — I noticed [relevant observation].

I am testing a simple offer for [target audience]: I will help [specific person/team] [specific result] in [timeframe].

If it works, [testimonial/payment condition]. If not, [risk reversal].

Worth trying?

### Template 2: Can I Send You? Asset

Subject: [asset topic]

[First name] — I made a short [asset] for [target audience] dealing with [pain].

It shows [specific thing they will get].

Want me to send it over?

### Template 3: Teardown / Diagnostic

Subject: [specific question]

[First name] — I had a thought on [company/team/problem].

I can record a quick teardown showing [specific issue/opportunity] and the 2-3 fixes I would make.

Worth sending?

### Template 4: Testimonial Exchange

Subject: [result question]

[First name] — I am opening a few trial spots for [target audience].

I will help you [specific result] over [timeframe]. If it creates real momentum, give me a testimonial. If not, you owe me nothing.

Interested?

### Template 5: Contrarian Opener

Subject: [problem]

[First name] — my guess is [target audience] rarely needs more advice.

They need someone to help them finish the uncomfortable work they already know they are avoiding.

I am testing a 30-day sprint for that. Want details?

## Follow-Up Rules

A follow-up should stand alone. Do not say:

- Just following up
- Bumping this
- I know you are busy
- I sent you an email last week

Instead, add one new value point, reduce friction, or pre-handle an objection.

Follow-up categories:

1. Add proof
2. Add specificity
3. Reduce effort
4. Explain why free
5. Offer a lighter asset
6. Switch CTA from "try this" to "want me to send a 2-minute video?"

## Follow-Up Templates

### Follow-up 1: Add Value Point

Also [first name], worth mentioning: this is not extra strategy work.

The goal is to turn the action items already sitting in your head into shipped decisions, owner names, and deadlines.

Want me to send the format?

### Follow-up 2: Reduce Friction

Also [first name], this would take almost no prep.

You bring the messy list of things you and your cofounder keep postponing. I will help turn it into a 30-day execution sprint.

Worth a try?

### Follow-up 3: Switch to Asset

Maybe easier: I can send you the founder action-item scorecard I use to spot where teams are stuck.

Want it?

## Quality Checklist

Before final output, check:

- [ ] Is the offer different from the main service?
- [ ] Does it answer "what do I get if I reply?"
- [ ] Is there a specific result?
- [ ] Is there a timeframe?
- [ ] Is the risk low?
- [ ] Is the CTA easy?
- [ ] Is the email short enough to read in 10 seconds?
- [ ] Does it avoid generic coaching/consulting language?
- [ ] Does it create curiosity without being vague?
- [ ] Could the recipient simply reply "yes"?

## Final Response Format

When responding to the user, provide:

1. Best offer recommendation
2. 3-5 alternative offers
3. Best first email
4. 2 follow-ups
5. Notes on why the offer works
6. Optional subject lines

## Example for Founder Coaching

### Best Offer

I will help your founding team clear the decisions, conversations, and action items you have been avoiding for 30 days. If it creates real momentum, give me a testimonial. If not, you owe me nothing.

### Why It Works

- It does not sell coaching
- It sells relief from avoided work
- It has a clear 30-day container
- It names a painful founder reality
- It explains why the offer is free
- It creates a "why not?" reaction

### Email

Subject: avoided action items

[First name] — quick thought.

I am testing a 30-day sprint for 2-founder teams.

I will help you and your cofounder clear the decisions, conversations, and action items you have been avoiding. If it creates real momentum, give me a testimonial. If not, you owe me nothing.

Worth trying?

## Core Pipeline

Always move through this sequence:

service -> painful avoided problem -> tangible front-end offer -> risk reversal -> low-friction CTA -> short email -> value-adding follow-up
